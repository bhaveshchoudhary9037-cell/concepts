Concept #1

The Purple Cow

Coined by Seth Godin, the Purple Cow concept is about creating something truly remarkable — so extraordinary that people can't help but talk about it. In a field of ordinary brown and white cows, a purple cow stands out instantly.

The core idea is that being safe is risky. If your product, service, or marketing doesn't stand out, it's invisible. Godin argues that traditional advertising (TV, radio, print) has become "brown noise" — background clutter that consumers tune out.

To create a Purple Cow, you need to build remarkable products from the start — not just add remarkable marketing to an ordinary product. Think of how Apple redesigned product packaging, or how Dollar Shave Club disrupted with humor.

Concept #2

The AIDA Model

The AIDA Model is one of the oldest and most foundational marketing frameworks, dating back to 1898. It describes the stages a consumer goes through before making a purchase decision.

Each stage requires different marketing strategies. An effective campaign must guide the prospect smoothly from one stage to the next, never losing momentum. The funnel narrows at each stage — not everyone who notices will buy, but the goal is to maximize conversions at every level.

Modern digital marketing has adapted AIDA for online environments: attention through SEO/ads, interest through content, desire through social proof and personalization, and action through optimized CTAs and checkout flows.

Build emotional connection through benefits, testimonials, and FOMO

Action

Drive conversion with clear CTAs, limited offers, and frictionless checkout

Concept #3

The Halo Effect

When one positive quality creates an overwhelming positive impression of the whole

What is the Halo Effect?

The Halo Effect is a cognitive bias where our overall impression of a person, brand, or product influences how we feel and think about their character or qualities. First identified by psychologist Edward Thorndike in 1920, it explains why attractive people are often assumed to be smarter, or why a beautifully designed product is assumed to be higher quality.

🍎

Apple Example

Apple's sleek store design creates a halo that makes all their products feel premium, even before you try them.

🏋️

Athlete Endorsements

A top athlete wearing Nike shoes transfers their performance excellence to the brand in consumers' minds.

🏨

Hospitality

A luxurious lobby makes guests assume the rooms, food, and service are equally excellent.

📱

Social Proof

A brand with many followers is perceived as more trustworthy and higher quality than unknown brands.

How to Leverage the Halo Effect

→

→

Create consistency across all touchpoints

Concept #5

The Flywheel Effect

Popularized by Jim Collins and adopted by Amazon and HubSpot, the Flywheel Effect describes how momentum builds over time through consistent effort.

Unlike a funnel (where customers enter one end and exit another), a flywheel is a self-reinforcing loop. Each rotation builds energy. Satisfied customers become promoters who attract new customers, who become satisfied, and the cycle accelerates.

The key insight: remove friction and add energy. Every point of customer friction slows the wheel. Every delight moment adds energy. Over time, even small optimizations compound into massive momentum.

Happy customers refer others — spinning the wheel faster

Concept #6

The Law of Category

From Al Ries & Jack Trout, the Law of Category states that when you can't be first in an existing category, create a new category you can be first in.

Instead of trying to beat the leader in their game, redefine the game entirely. Every category leader was once a leader of a new category they created. The first gets the most attention, the most trial, and the most loyalty.

Being the "better" option rarely works against an established leader. But being the "only" option in a category you invented — that's the winning strategy.

📖 The Theory

Named after comedian Lindy's observation about Broadway shows: a show that's been running for 100 days is likely to run another 100. A show that's been running 1 day might only last 1 more. The life expectancy of non-perishable things (ideas, brands, technologies) is proportional to their current age.

🎯 Marketing Implication

Build brands that stand the test of time. Focus on timeless principles over fleeting trends. Legacy brands have compounding trust. Invest in heritage, authenticity, and traditions that reinforce longevity. Old brands should leverage their age as proof of reliability.

Concept #8

Product-Market Fit

Coined by Marc Andreessen, Product-Market Fit (PMF) means being in a good market with a product that can satisfy that market. It's the single most important condition for startup success.

PMF is when your product solves a real problem for a specific group of people so well that they can't imagine going back to life without it. Sean Ellis's test: "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?" If more than 40% say "very disappointed," you likely have PMF.

Signs of PMF include organic growth, high retention, customers doing marketing for you, and sales growing faster than you can handle.

✓ Organic word-of-mouth growth
✓ High customer retention & usage
✓ Sales growing faster than ability to serve
✓ Customers say they'd be "very disappointed" without it
✓ Press coverage happens organically

"Do whatever is required to make the product work until product-market fit. Once you achieve PMF, you can then focus on scaling."

— Marc Andreessen

Concept #10

Creating Sales Funnels

The systematic process of turning strangers into paying customers through strategic nurturing at each stage

1

Top of Funnel (TOFU) — Awareness

Cast a wide net to attract potential customers. They don't know you yet — your job is to get on their radar.

SEO & Blog Content Social Media Ads Podcasts YouTube
2

Middle of Funnel (MOFU) — Interest & Consideration

Nurture leads with valuable content. Build trust and educate them about your solution.

Email Sequences Lead Magnets Webinars Case Studies
3

Bottom of Funnel (BOFU) — Intent & Purchase

Convert leads into customers with compelling offers, demos, and urgency.

Free Trials Demos Discounts Testimonials Retargeting
✓

Post-Purchase — Retention & Advocacy

The funnel doesn't end at purchase. Retain customers and turn them into promoters who feed new leads back to the top.

Onboarding Support Upsells Referral Programs Loyalty Rewards

Pro Tip: The best sales funnels don't just move people through — they also optimize each transition. Track your conversion rates between stages, A/B test messaging, and constantly remove friction. Even a 1% improvement at each stage compounds dramatically.
